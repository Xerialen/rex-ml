# SPEC F1.2 — RL on top of the behaviour-cloned policy

## Why

Everything phase 0 built exists to make this run honest: the observation matches the policy contract,
the Rust forward pass matches torch exactly, wall contact is counted the way the owner's acceptance
runner counts it, and the architecture is the largest the tick budget allows. The environment now
plans real navmesh routes and steps them in batches.

What BC gives us is a policy that agrees with humans on about 68 % of ticks. What it does not give us
is a policy that *completes routes fast*, because imitation has no notion of arriving. That is what
RL is for.

## The first thing to measure, before any training conclusion

`evidence/f1.1_vecenv.json` reports the Python boundary retaining 2–5 % of the environment's native
throughput (6.85 M steps/s at N = 16384 against 139.5 M native), and concludes the rollout loop
should move into Rust.

**Do not act on that conclusion. Test it.** The retained fraction is the wrong question; the right
one is whether the environment or the torch learner is the *binding* constraint end to end. 6.85 M
steps/s is roughly 89,000× realtime. Report, for a real training step at N = 4096 and N = 16384:

- seconds spent in `env.step()` per iteration;
- seconds spent in the policy forward pass during rollout;
- seconds spent in the backward pass and optimiser;
- the resulting end-to-end steps/s.

Then state plainly which one dominates. If the environment is not the constraint, moving it into Rust
would optimise the part that is not binding, and we will not do it.

## Training

- PPO (or a justified alternative — say why if you deviate).
- **Warm start from `pipeline/out/policy/actor_disc_3x512.pt`.** The value function has no warm start
  and must be learned; expect the first iterations to be dominated by value error, and do not read
  early policy collapse as a bug before checking whether the advantage estimates are simply noise.
- The action space is the policy's own: 3-way `fwd`, 3-way `side`, continuous `dyaw`, binary `jump`.
- Reward weights are **data, not constants** (`RewardWeights` already models this). Run at least
  three weightings and report which reward term the policy actually responded to. The five terms
  come back separately from the environment for exactly this reason — do not sum them in the
  environment.

## Routes

Route coordinates are the owner's contract and are not to be invented:
`~/rtx-mltest/testsuite/scenarios/dm3/*.toml`, each with `start`, `target` and `timeout_s`. Use
every `kind = "goto"` dm3 scenario whose route constructs successfully via `Route::planned`.

**Report which scenarios fail to construct and why.** A route whose endpoints do not snap is a real
finding about the navmesh, not something to skip quietly — `rex-env-bench`'s own goal already turned
out not to snap.

Do not train on `dash_100m` (it is a straight runway, not a route) or on any `rj_*` scenario (rocket
jumps are out of scope for the movement policy and the environment has no rocket launcher).

## What to report per route

For the final policy, over **≥ 30 episodes per route**:

| quantity | why |
|---|---|
| arrival rate | a fast median over 3 arrivals is meaningless |
| median completion time (s) | the gate |
| p90 completion time | spread has been the bot's real problem, not the median |
| **fraction of moving ticks above 320 u/s** | the bunny-hop gate, G1.5 — target ≥ 50 % |
| median horizontal speed | human reference is 331 u/s |
| wall-contact episodes | the acceptance runner requires zero |

The speed columns are not decoration. The owner's finding is that these routes are speed-gated —
without sustained bunny-hopping the bot cannot reach the takeoffs at all and adds ~20 s of detour.
A route "passed" on time but below the speed floor has not been learned, it has been walked.
