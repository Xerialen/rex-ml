# SPEC G0.1 — `rex-env` observation must match the trained policy's contract

## Why

`rtx/crates/rex-env/src/lib.rs` defines an 8-field `Obs` that was invented before anyone read
`pipeline/out/policy/policy.json`. The exported policy (`policy.bin`, 14→256→256→8) takes a
**14-dimensional** input in a fixed order with fixed scales. Feeding it the current `Obs` produces
confident, wrong actions — the failure is silent, which is exactly why this is the first gate.

There is a second, larger mismatch. In training (`pipeline/policy.py:99-117`) the goal is **not the
route endpoint** — it is a point on the human's own track `H` ticks ahead, `H` drawn uniformly from
**15..60 ticks (~0.2–0.8 s)**. The policy is a *local waypoint follower*. `rex-env` currently feeds
the final goal, often thousands of units away, far outside the training distribution.

## Scope

Change **only** `rtx/crates/rex-env/`. Do not touch `rtx-nav`, `rtx-game`, or anything under
`bot/`. Do not run `cargo fmt` (repo protocol forbids it in this tree).

## 1. The observation, exactly

`Obs` becomes a `[f32; 14]` (plus named accessors if you like, but the array order **is** the
contract). Source of truth: `pipeline/out/policy/policy.json` (`state_cols`, `s_scale`) and
`pipeline/policy.py:132-138`. Each field is divided by its scale.

| i | field | scale | definition |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | `v_fwd` | 400 | `vx*cos(yaw) + vy*sin(yaw)` |
| 1 | `v_right` | 400 | `vx*sin(yaw) − vy*cos(yaw)` |
| 2 | `vz` | 400 | vertical velocity |
| 3 | `speed_xy` | 400 | `hypot(vx, vy)` |
| 4 | `slip_sin` | 1 | `sin(atan2(v_right, v_fwd))` |
| 5 | `slip_cos` | 1 | `cos(atan2(v_right, v_fwd))` |
| 6 | `omega_prev` | 10 | turn rate **in rad/s** that produced this tick: `dyaw_prev / dt` |
| 7 | `pitch` | 90 | view pitch, degrees |
| 8 | `on_ground` | 1 | 1.0 when standing on ground |
| 9 | `was_air` | 1 | 1.0 when the **previous** tick was airborne |
| 10 | `goal_f` | 500 | `dx*cos(yaw) + dy*sin(yaw)` — an **offset in units**, not a unit vector |
| 11 | `goal_r` | 500 | `dx*sin(yaw) − dy*cos(yaw)` |
| 12 | `goal_z` | 200 | goal z − own z |
| 13 | `goal_dist` | 500 | `sqrt(goal_f² + goal_r² + goal_z²)` — **3D**, not planar |

Notes that matter:

- The body basis is Quake's **left-handed** horizontal view basis: `e_f = (cos, sin)`,
  `e_r = (sin, −cos)`. It is already correct in the current `basis()`; keep it. Getting it wrong is
  silent — it preserves speeds and only bends directions.
- `slip_sin`/`slip_cos` reduce to `v_right/speed_xy` and `v_fwd/speed_xy` when moving. At
  `speed_xy == 0`, `atan2(0,0) == 0`, so they must come out **0.0 and 1.0**. Do not emit NaN.
- `omega_prev` is **radians per second**, and the training pipeline replaced NaN with 0.0
  (`policy.py:135`). First tick of an episode is 0.0.
- `pitch` is **always 0.0** here: `rex-env` is a movement-only environment with no view pitch.
  Document this in a comment as a known deviation from the training distribution — it is a real one
  and must be visible in the code, not buried.

## 2. `dyaw` is radians, not degrees

`step()` currently takes `dyaw` in degrees and adds it to a degrees `view_yaw`. The policy's yaw
head decodes as `tanh(out[6]) * 0.35` and the training target was `wrap_pi(yaw[i+1] − yaw[i])` in
**radians per tick** (`se2.py:164`). Make the env's `dyaw` radians per tick. Keep whatever internal
representation you like, but the public `step` signature must be in the policy's units, and
`omega_prev` must be `dyaw / TICK_DT`.

## 3. The goal becomes a local waypoint

- `Route` gains `path: Vec<Vec3>`. When a caller supplies only `start`/`goal`, the path is
  `vec![start, goal]`.
- `Route` gains `goal_horizon_s: f32`, default **0.5** (the middle of the 0.2–0.8 s training range).
- The observed goal is the point on the path at arclength `speed_xy * goal_horizon_s` ahead of the
  agent's closest point on the path, clamped to the path's end. When `speed_xy` is near zero use a
  floor of **64 units** of lookahead so a standing start still has a direction to move in.
- **Arrival is still judged against the final path point**, with the existing `arrive_box`. The
  lookahead changes what the policy *sees*, never what counts as success.

## 4. Tests you must add

In `rex-env`, all deterministic, no server, no map required where possible:

1. `slip` at zero speed gives exactly `(0.0, 1.0)`.
2. Body-frame round trip: with `yaw = 90°` and world velocity `(0, 320, 0)`, `v_fwd == 320` and
   `v_right == 0` (within 1e-4).
3. `goal_dist` is the 3D norm of fields 10–12, not the planar one.
4. `omega_prev` equals the previous tick's `dyaw / TICK_DT`, and is 0.0 on the first tick.
5. Lookahead: on a straight 1000-unit path with the agent at the start at 400 u/s and
   `goal_horizon_s = 0.5`, the observed goal is 200 units ahead — **not** the far endpoint.
6. Lookahead clamps to the path end rather than running past it.

## Done when

`cargo test -p rex-env` passes, `cargo build -p rex-env --release` is clean, and the 14 fields come
out in the documented order and scales. Do **not** attempt the Python↔Rust parity run (that is G0.2,
a separate task with a separate spec).

## Reporting

Report what you changed, the test names, and — explicitly — anything in this spec you found to be

# REVIEW ROUND 1 (spec owner, 2026-07-28)

Implementation accepted on the contract itself: field order, scales, left-handed basis, radian
`dyaw`, and `was_air` all verified against `policy.json` and `policy.py` independently. Two things
to fix, then this is done.

## Fix 1 — `Route::goal` and `path.last()` must not be able to disagree

You flagged it and you were right. A caller who builds a multi-point `path` without updating `goal`
gets an observation sliding toward one point while arrival is judged against another — and nothing
would report it. Make it impossible rather than merely unlikely: have the constructor take the path
and *derive* `goal` from its last point, or assert the invariant. Your choice, but a caller must not
be able to express the inconsistent state.

## Fix 2 — the closest-point search will jump backwards on a route that passes near itself

This is the one to take seriously, and it is not the O(n) cost you flagged — it is correctness.
`closest_arclength` searches the **whole** path for the globally nearest point. Several of the DM3
routes we are graded on double back past earlier geometry (the RA climb passes under itself; the
ring routes loop). When the agent is near such a crossing, the nearest point can be on a *later*
segment, and the lookahead teleports the goal far down the path — or on an *earlier* one, and the
goal snaps backwards. Either way the policy gets a goal from a part of the route it is not on, and
the failure looks like bad steering rather than a bad observation.

Make progress along the path monotone: remember the arclength matched last tick and search only a
local window forward of it (a few hundred units is plenty at these speeds), resetting on `reset()`.
That is standard pure-pursuit behaviour and it also removes the O(path) cost as a side effect.

Add a test with a path that folds back on itself — e.g. out along +X and back along a line 48 units
away — where an agent near the fold would match the wrong segment under a global search, and assert
the matched arclength advances monotonically.

## Accepted as-is, no change needed

- `was_air == 0.0` at reset: your inference matches `policy.py:129-130`, where the first row of a
  track is `False` by construction.
- 3D arclength rather than planar: correct, and I should have specified it. The training goal
  carries `goal_z` as its own feature precisely because routes climb.

## Recorded as a known deviation, deliberately not "fixed"

At rest the training goal is the human's own position 15–60 ticks later, which for a stationary
player is ~0 units away, while `MIN_LOOKAHEAD` feeds 64. That is a real difference from the training
distribution. Keeping it: a goal 64 units ahead is a normal move-forward signal, whereas a zero goal
would ask the policy to reproduce standing still, which is the one behaviour this environment must
never reward. Leave the constant, but say this in the comment on `MIN_LOOKAHEAD` so the next reader
knows it is a choice and not an accident.
