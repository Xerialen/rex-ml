# SPEC K0.2 — a learned enemy-motion predictor that beats constant velocity

## Why

rtx aims by assuming the enemy holds its current velocity (`bot/combat/aim.rs::intercept_time`,
`ballistic_pos`). On held-out demos that assumption is off by **44.7 u at the median rocket flight
time**, measured in `evidence/k0_baseline.json`:

| horizon | still (median / p90) | linear = what rtx does (median / p90) |
|---|---|---|
| 300 ms | 97.4 / 143.8 u | **44.7 / 106.4 u** |
| 600 ms | 168.5 / 272.0 u | **126.9 / 281.4 u** |

The player hull is 32 u wide, so the analytic lead misses by more than a hull at typical engagement
range. At 600 ms linear extrapolation is *worse than assuming the enemy stands still* in the p90 —
projecting a bunny-hopping player half a second forward on a straight line can throw the aim further
off than not projecting at all. That is the opening this task tests.

This is the **kill criterion for the whole ML-combat track**. If a learned predictor cannot beat
constant velocity by a margin that changes hit outcomes, K1–K3 are called off. Report the honest
number; a failure here is a valid and valuable result, not a task to be rescued.

## The gate

| quantity | threshold |
|---|---|
| median error at 300 ms | **<= 28.7 u** (44.7 − 16) |
| median error at 600 ms | **< 126.9 u** (strictly better than linear) |
| p90 error at 600 ms | **< 272.0 u** (must at least beat *standing still*) |

16 u is not arbitrary: it is `fire_tolerance(skill=7, direct=true)` in `bot/combat/aim.rs` — the
distance below which a change in the aim point does not change whether a direct hit lands.

## Data

- `~/dm3-extract/store-dm3/trajectory_samples`, partitioned `split=train|val|test`. **Train on
  `train` only. Tune on `val`. Report on `test` only, once.**
- The table has **no velocity at all**: `vx/vy/vz` are null throughout and `velocity_present` is
  false everywhere. Only `x,y,z` positions and `vp`/`vya` view angles. Velocity must be
  finite-differenced from positions — which is also what a bot must do against an enemy it sees only
  over the wire, so this is realistic rather than a handicap.
- Median sample spacing is 16 ms (~62 Hz), p90 51 ms — **irregular**. Do not assume a fixed rate;
  carry `dt` explicitly or resample.
- 8 slots per demo in the great majority of demos, so all players are observable, not just the POV.

`/tmp/claude-1001/-home-benjamin-adm-rex-ml/003dd697-8855-417d-9d80-53960851ebcf/scratchpad/k0_baseline.py`
computes the baselines; reuse its windowing (velocity differenced over an 8–40 ms gap, target sample
within ±25 ms of `t+H`) so your numbers are comparable to it. If you change the windowing, re-run
the baselines under the new windowing and report both.

## What to build

`pipeline/predict_enemy.py`, following the conventions of the existing pipeline modules
(`pipeline/policy.py` is the closest model — read it first).

Suggested input features, but you own this choice and should report what you tried:

- a short history of positions in a **body/track-relative frame**, not world coordinates — absolute
  position lets a model memorise dm3 geometry instead of learning motion;
- the differenced velocity and its recent change;
- **the target's own view angles `vp`/`vya`.** These are the feature the analytic model has no way
  to use, and in QuakeWorld a player accelerates roughly where they look. If anything is going to
  beat constant velocity, this is the most likely reason.

Predict the **offset** from the linear extrapolation, not the absolute position. Then the model
starts at parity with the baseline and has only the residual to learn, and a failed run degrades to
the baseline instead of to nonsense.

## The reason for another round: the plateau is not established

You trained on **5,856,984 rows from 300 demos** and evaluated on 39,040,944. That is **0.8 % of the
807 M rows available**, and the H100 sat mostly idle. Three configurations converging at one data
scale tells us about that regime; it does not establish a ceiling for the problem. The owner's
standing instruction is to use the machine to the maximum for the best result, and a ceiling claim
resting on 0.8 % of the corpus does not meet that bar.

Re-run with **at least 60 M training rows**, drawn from many more demos rather than more rows from
the same 300 — demo diversity is what covers map geometry and player styles, and 300 demos is a
narrow slice of 2,449. Scale the model with the data rather than keeping 70 k parameters: you have
19x headroom against the 0.5 ms per-tick budget and 95 GB of GPU memory, so width and depth are
nearly free here. Report the parameter count and keep it plausible for the budget, which K0.4 checks
separately.

Disk: caches for that many rows land in the several-GB range. There is 179 GB free. Stay under 20 GB
and say what you used.

Do **not** touch the test split again until the final measurement, and report val numbers as val.

## The gate itself is under review — do not optimise toward it

I derived the 16 u margin from `fire_tolerance(skill = 7, direct = true)`. That is the **direct-hit**
standard, and on reflection it is the wrong one for this problem: enemy-motion prediction only
matters for *projectile* weapons, and the dominant one is the rocket, whose fire gate in the same
function is `fire_tolerance(skill = 7, direct = false)` = **40 u**. The gate may move. Report the raw
error distribution — median, p90, and the fraction of rows under 16 u, 40 u and 160 u (the splash
radius) — and let the threshold question be settled on the numbers rather than baked into training.
